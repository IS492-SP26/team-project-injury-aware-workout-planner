# User Interviews

To validate the idea behind the **Injury-Aware Workout Planner**, we conducted interviews with individuals representing both **medical expertise** and **real user experience**. The goal was to understand whether people would trust AI-assisted workout adaptation and what concerns or expectations might arise.


## Interview 1: Neha Agarwal  
**Role:** Medical Professional – Senior Resident

I discussed whether medical professionals would be comfortable with people using AI tools to generate exercise recommendations.

### Key Insights

- Doctors generally recommend **physiotherapy-based movements rather than heavy workouts** when a person is recovering from an injury.
- She emphasized the importance of **including medical disclaimers** before suggesting any exercise recommendations.
- From a medical perspective, AI-assisted guidance can be acceptable **if proper safety precautions and disclaimers are included**.
- She noted that many people will likely **rely on AI tools for guidance**, which makes it important for such systems to be designed responsibly.
- After hearing the concept and workflow of the application, she mentioned that the idea is **novel and interesting**, and she would be curious to see how the final product develops.

### Takeaway

Medical professionals may be open to AI-assisted tools **as long as they emphasize safety, include disclaimers, and prioritize physiotherapy-style exercises over intense workouts during recovery**.


## Interview 2: Aman Sharma  
**Role:** Gym Enthusiast with Shoulder Injury Experience

To understand the user perspective, we interviewed a regular gym-goer who had previously experienced shoulder injuries.

### Key Insights

- During his injury recovery, he mainly relied on **light exercises and physiotherapy movements rather than intense workouts**.
- This suggested that future versions of the application should **include physiotherapy-oriented alternatives alongside workout substitutions**.
- One of the biggest challenges during injury recovery is **figuring out which exercises to skip or modify in workout videos**.
- Currently, users often need to **manually search for safer alternatives and adjust workouts themselves**.
- He mentioned that a tool that automatically **flags risky exercises and suggests safer options** would be very helpful.

### Takeaway

Users recovering from injuries would benefit from a system that **automatically identifies unsafe exercises and suggests safer alternatives**, removing the need to manually modify workouts.
