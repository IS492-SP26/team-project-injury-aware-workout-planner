## Survey Overview

The AdaptFit AI post-task evaluation survey was designed to collect participant feedback after reviewing the prototype/workflow. The survey focuses on users’ understanding of the tool, perceived usability, usefulness, safety framing, trust, and suggestions for improvement.

The survey includes both **Likert-scale rating questions** and **open-ended feedback questions**. Quantitative responses were used to summarize overall user perceptions, while qualitative responses were used to identify recurring feedback themes and product improvement opportunities.

---

## Survey Structure

### Section 1: Safety Framing and Product Understanding

This section evaluates whether participants understood AdaptFit AI’s intended role and safety boundaries. Questions in this section focus on whether users recognized the tool as a workout support assistant rather than a replacement for medical advice, and whether the workflow clearly communicated when users should pause exercise or seek professional support.

**Topics covered:**

- Understanding of AdaptFit AI as a support tool
- Clarity of medical/safety boundaries
- Overall workflow clarity
- Potential confusion around whether the app is for planning, coaching, or workout logging

---

### Section 2: Injury Assessment and Recommendation Quality

This section evaluates whether the injury-related inputs and generated recommendations felt relevant and appropriate. Questions focus on the quality of the injury assessment, the usefulness of risk labels, and whether safer exercise substitutions were explained clearly enough.

**Topics covered:**

- Relevance of injury assessment questions
- Appropriateness of risk labels
- Appropriateness of safer exercise substitutions
- Explanation quality for risky exercises and recommended alternatives

---

### Section 3: Usefulness and Trust

This section evaluates whether participants found AdaptFit AI practically useful and trustworthy for injury-aware workout modification. Questions focus on whether the app reduced the effort needed to decide which exercises to skip or modify, and whether users would trust the tool in mid-stage recovery contexts when appropriate caution messages are included.

**Topics covered:**

- Reduced effort in workout modification decisions
- Need for additional trainer or clinician support
- User confidence in applying recommendations
- Conditional trust in AdaptFit AI for safer workout planning

---

### Section 4: Open-Ended Feedback

This section collects qualitative feedback about participants’ experience with the prototype. The open-ended questions ask users to identify the most helpful part of the tool, the least reliable or riskiest part, and the most important improvement for the next version.

**Topics covered:**

- Most helpful feature or workflow step
- Least reliable or most risky part of the experience
- Suggested priority for future improvement
- User concerns, expectations, and product opportunity areas

---

## Evaluation Metrics Captured

The survey was designed to capture the following evaluation dimensions:

| Evaluation Area | Related Survey Focus |
|---|---|
| Safety framing | Whether users understood the tool’s non-medical support role |
| Product understanding | Whether users understood what the app is designed to do |
| Workflow usability | Whether the flow was easy to follow |
| Injury assessment relevance | Whether the assessment questions matched user injury context |
| Recommendation quality | Whether risk labels and substitutions felt appropriate |
| Explanation quality | Whether users understood why an exercise was risky and why an alternative was suggested |
| Usefulness | Whether the tool reduced effort in workout modification decisions |
| Trust | Whether users would rely on the tool with appropriate caution |
| Qualitative feedback | Open-ended themes around helpful features, risks, and improvement priorities |

---

## Notes for Analysis

Survey responses were analyzed using descriptive statistics for the Likert-scale questions, including mean, standard deviation, and top-2 box agreement. Open-ended responses were reviewed and coded into recurring qualitative themes.

This evaluation did not collect formal task completion rate, time-on-task, system error rate, SUS, UMUX-Lite, or baseline comparison data. These measures could be added in future evaluations to provide a more complete usability and system performance assessment.
