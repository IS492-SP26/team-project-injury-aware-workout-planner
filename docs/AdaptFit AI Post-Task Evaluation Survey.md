## Survey Overview

The AdaptFit AI post-task evaluation survey was designed to collect participant feedback after reviewing the prototype/workflow. The survey focuses on users’ understanding of the tool, perceived usability, usefulness, safety framing, trust, and suggestions for improvement.

The survey includes **participant background questions**, **Likert-scale rating questions**, and **open-ended feedback questions**. Quantitative responses were used to summarize overall user perceptions, while qualitative responses were used to identify recurring feedback themes and product improvement opportunities.

---

## Survey Structure

### Section 0: Participant Background

This section collects basic participant context to help interpret feedback across different user perspectives. These questions help identify whether participants had medical or rehabilitation-related knowledge, as well as whether they had personal workout experience that could influence how they evaluated the tool.

**Questions included:**

1. Are you a medical professional? If yes, add details.
2. Are you a frequent gym-goer? If yes, describe frequency and experience.

---

### Section 1: Safety Framing and Product Understanding

This section evaluates whether participants understood AdaptFit AI’s intended role and safety boundaries. It focuses on whether users recognized the tool as a workout support assistant rather than a replacement for medical advice, and whether the workflow clearly communicated when users should pause exercise or seek professional support.

**Questions included:**

3. I understood that AdaptFit AI is a support tool, not a replacement for medical advice.
4. The app made it clear when a user should pause exercise and consult a professional.
5. The workflow was easy to follow.
6. I felt confused about whether the app was for planning, coaching, or logging workouts.

---

### Section 2: Injury Assessment and Recommendation Quality

This section evaluates whether the injury-related inputs and generated recommendations felt relevant and appropriate. It focuses on the quality of the injury assessment, the usefulness of risk labels, and whether safer exercise substitutions were explained clearly enough.

**Questions included:**

7. The injury assessment questions felt relevant to the user’s condition.
8. The risk labels and suggested exercise substitutions seemed appropriate.
9. The app provided enough explanation for why an exercise may be risky and why a safer alternative was recommended.

---

### Section 3: Usefulness and Trust

This section evaluates whether participants found AdaptFit AI practically useful and trustworthy for injury-aware workout modification. It focuses on whether the app reduced the effort needed to decide which exercises to skip or modify, and whether users would trust the tool in mid-stage recovery contexts when appropriate caution messages are included.

**Questions included:**

10. I would still need substantial help from a trainer or clinician to use the recommendations confidently.
11. The app reduced the effort required to figure out which exercises to skip or modify.
12. I would trust AdaptFit AI to support safer mid-stage recovery workouts, as long as it includes appropriate caution messages.

---

### Section 4: Open-Ended Feedback

This section collects qualitative feedback about participants’ experience with the prototype. The open-ended questions ask users to identify the most helpful part of the tool, the least reliable or riskiest part, and the most important improvement for the next version.

**Questions included:**

13. What was the single most helpful part of AdaptFit AI?

14. What felt least reliable or most risky?

15. What should be improved first?

---

## Rating Scale

For the Likert-scale questions, participants responded using a 5-point agreement scale:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Strongly disagree |
| 2 | Disagree |
| 3 | Neutral |
| 4 | Agree |
| 5 | Strongly agree |

---

## Evaluation Metrics Captured

The survey was designed to capture the following evaluation dimensions:

| Evaluation Area | Related Survey Focus |
|---|---|
| Participant background | Medical/rehabilitation knowledge and workout experience |
| Safety framing | Whether users understood the tool’s non-medical support role |
| Product understanding | Whether users understood what the app is designed to do |
| Workflow usability | Whether the flow was easy to follow |
| Injury assessment relevance | Whether the assessment questions matched user's injury context |
| Recommendation quality | Whether risk labels and substitutions felt appropriate |
| Explanation quality | Whether users understood why an exercise was risky and why an alternative was suggested |
| Usefulness | Whether the tool reduced effort in workout modification decisions |
| Trust | Whether users would rely on the tool with appropriate caution |
| Qualitative feedback | Open-ended themes around helpful features, risks, and improvement priorities |

---

## Notes for Analysis

Survey responses were analyzed using descriptive statistics for Likert-scale questions, including mean, standard deviation, and top-2-box agreement. Open-ended responses were reviewed and coded into recurring qualitative themes.

Participant background questions were used to contextualize feedback from different perspectives, including users with medical or rehabilitation-related knowledge and users with regular gym or athletic experience.

Negatively worded items, such as confusion about the app’s purpose and a need for substantial external help, should be interpreted carefully, as lower scores indicate more positive outcomes.

This evaluation did not collect formal task completion rate, time-on-task, system error rate, SUS, UMUX-Lite, or baseline comparison data. These measures could be added in future evaluations to provide a more complete usability and system performance assessment.
